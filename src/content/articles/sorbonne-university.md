Since 2024, I've been working as a tour guide in Paris. It's a job that I love - I get to meet so many different people, I spend my days telling stories, and every day is different. Paris has so much to offer, and there really is something for everyone in this city.

### Why did I start studying?

In France, in order to give tours inside museums, you need to have a guiding licence. There are a few different ways of getting this, and getting a degree in history or arts history is one path. I'm fascinated by history, it's a big part of my job so this seemed like a great opportunity. I had spent the 2025-2026 year getting my French high school diploma in preparation for this (I did the [DAEU](/articles/highschool-in-france/)). I was SO EXCITED to start studying. I had told _everyone_ about it, but a few weeks in, I have realised that this is not the right path for me at this period of my life.

### The student strikes

My lessons started on the 14th September. More or less the entire first week was dedicated to an introduction of each course - the structure, the expectations, the types of exams, which all vary between each class. In total I had 11 classes per week (plus homework for each lesson). The second week was the first week of real lessons. And by the third week, the 29th of September, Sorbonne was already impacted by the student strikes.

It's incredible to see how the students come to together to show their anger, their frustration, and how they want to see things change. They really are fighting for change, and it's so impressive to see. I cannot imagine something like this happening in the UK. I wanted to join the movement, but I felt like an imposter. I am aware that this is all in my head, and that they would have welcomed me in. But I am not French (yet), I cannot vote, and I still don't feel 100% comfortable in French for situations like this. And I was also thinking about the studying, not wanting to fall behind. While I have more life experience than the average student, I had to work much harder because of the language barrier. Reading in French takes me longer, and requires more concentration.

So, from the start I was already conflicted. Do I study? Do I participate? Should I attend my online lessons? Because I really do believe in the movement. I'm living through a major moment, and yet I'm not participating _and_ I'm worried about my grades. People are comparing this to _the_ protests of May 68. Is that how I want to live my life?

One of the big things that the students at Sorbonne are fighting for, is the tuition fees for international students. They think that international students should not have to pay more than French students. Education is so important (it doesn't just need to be in an academic setting), and should be accessible. I benefited for the French student rate because I had been living in France to qualify. University here is so much cheaper than in the UK which is why I did not go when I was 18 (it was £9k per year) so cost really is a deciding factor when it comes to pursing education. This was their main demand. But then the lycées (the school you attend when you're 15-18) were also protesting, and the movement became one of solidarity.

In week three and week four, I had essentially no classes. Two of my teachers did online lessons. The rest have been cancelled. Some teachers have made it very clear that they support the movement, and they are striking. They have warned about the violence from the police, and that our safety is important.

This is definitely one big reason why I decided to stop studying. I don't know how long the blockades are going to last. But I know that I cannot pass my exams without the lessons. Despite the inconvenience, I support the students. I hope the students are going to get better conditions. I think if the government does not react, and the protests stop, this will be a bad sign for the future. 2027 is going to be a big year for French politics because there is the presidential elections.

### Another project

The end goal is getting my guiding licence. There are ways of getting the guiding licence without getting a degree. In some ways, it is going to be harder, in other ways it's going to be easier. How would I like to spend my time? What brings me joy in life?

I've had this idea for a while (the domain was purchased over a year ago), and I've decided that now is the moment to work on it. But I cannot do that at the same time as studying. I'm working on a platform to book guided tours, where guides get a say in how the platform is ran, and they get paid fairly for what they do. Because believe it or not, a lot of guides do not get paid fairly for the work they do. I've been there. I want to change that.

### French

While I do speak French (I have a French high school diploma), my french writing is not up to scratch for a degree. I knew this going into the degree, and I was fully prepared to work on my French along side studying. My French would get better throughout the year from the exposure _and_ needing to use it - I can already notice the difference in the few weeks since I started. But I know that I am going to be penalised for my grammar. I make simple mistakes, I don't always use the _right_ word. I know it's going to make passing harder. So the language barrier, on top of not having lessons means there's a high change I'd need to resit the year. This turns my three year degree into a four year degree. Or turns the first six months of 2027 into a very intense period of studying. And I'm not sure if right now is the time for such a long term commitment.

I did question if dropping out was a sign of anxiety, or imposter syndrome. I spent a lot of time thinking about. Maybe part of the reason is because I'm still intimidated by speaking and writing in French. But the big reason is, I have decided to put my time and energy into another project.

### What's next?

Despite the fact I was only a student for five weeks, I have learn a lot. I have really thought about where my priorities are and how I want to spend my time. I love being in a classroom. I love learning. The love of learning is something that is very important when you're a tour guide.

So I want to keep that going - I want to find conferences around the city where I can learn something, deep dive into a topic or try something new. I want to participate in discussions related to the topic. If you know of any places that host regular or semi-regular events, i'd love to know! You can reach me via instagram at [@abi.in.france](https://www.instagram.com/abi.in.france/)! One of the things I really enjoyed in my classes, was learning about iconography. This is definitely something I'm going continue looking in to.

I'm definitely going to continue working on my French. I really do want to get to a C1 level. I think my understanding of spoken and written French is there, but I definitely still need to work on the speaking and writing part. Grammar is not something that I love, but I now understand how important it is so I will need to find the time to work on that alongside my guiding project. I'm going to continue reading books in French.

Despite deciding to drop out, I do not see this as a failure. Starting a degree in a language that is not your native is not easy. I got accepted to the Sorbonne university! How cool is that! I have learnt a lot, and it's helped me identify which route I want to take going forward.

I'm not saying I will never study, but right now, my life is taking me in another direction.
